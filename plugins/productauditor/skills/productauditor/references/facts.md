# Dated facts for product audits

Last reviewed: 2026-10-01. Every fact below says where to verify it; recheck before citing any fact older than six months.

Laws, provider APIs, and store rules change. The cards cite dated facts from this file only, so the cards do not go stale. Rules for using it:
- Cite a fact with this file's review date, for example "facts.md, reviewed 2026-10-01".
- A fact marked "(not verified at review)" could not be confirmed against its source. Confirm it before a finding depends on it, or write "(verify current status)" beside the citation and record the finding as Suspected.
- If the audit date is more than six months after the review date, say in Scope and limitations that the legal and provider facts may be stale, and prefer Likely or Suspected for findings that rest on them.
- Findings stand on a gap the code shows. A law only raises the stakes: Impact says "exposure under", never "violates". Never cite a vacated rule or a proposal as law.
- Provider facts depend on the SDK and API version the project pins (for Stripe, the `apiVersion` passed to the client, or the account default). Say which version you assumed.
- This file is not a full list of the laws that apply. When the product sells in a market this file does not cover, write the question in Scope and limitations.

## Subscriptions and cancellation in the United States

- FTC Negative Option Rule ("click-to-cancel"), announced 16 October 2024: vacated in full by the US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025 (Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC), before its main provisions took effect. Never cite it as law. The FTC restarted the rulemaking with an advance notice of proposed rulemaking published 13 March 2026 (comments closed 13 April 2026); no proposed or final rule had followed at review. Verify at: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule
- ROSCA, the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (2010, 15 U.S.C. 8401 to 8405): for online sales with a negative option, disclose all material terms clearly before taking billing information, get the consumer's express informed consent before charging, and provide a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges (15 U.S.C. 8403). The FTC enforces it with Section 5 of the FTC Act. Verify at: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/restore-online-shoppers-confidence-act
- FTC v. Amazon (Prime enrollment and cancellation, ROSCA and FTC Act): settled 25 September 2025 for 2.5 billion US dollars (a 1 billion civil penalty and 1.5 billion in refunds). It shows how the FTC reads hard cancellation and unclear enrollment after the rule was vacated. Verify at: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/amazoncom-inc-rosca-ftc-v-timeline-item-2025-09-25
- FTC Act Section 5 (15 U.S.C. 45) bans unfair or deceptive acts or practices. The FTC Policy Statement on Deception (14 October 1983) treats a material claim likely to mislead a reasonable consumer as deceptive. Verify at: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-deception
- FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation (1984): an objective claim about a product, such as a feature, a number, or a guarantee, needs a reasonable basis before it is made. Verify at: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation
- California Automatic Renewal Law (Business and Professions Code 17600 and following), as amended by AB 2863, applies to contracts entered, amended, or extended on or after 1 July 2025 (section 17602(j)). It covers free-to-paid conversions as well as paid renewals. It requires express affirmative consent to the renewal terms; cancellation through the same medium as sign-up, with a prominent "click to cancel" link or button for online sign-ups, and any save offer shown beside it (17602(e)); an annual reminder naming the product, the charge frequency and amount, and how to cancel (17602(h)); and records of consent kept for 3 years or 1 year after the contract ends, whichever is longer. Verify at: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17602
- California Automatic Renewal Law, notices of change: a clear and conspicuous notice of any material change in the terms, with how to cancel (17602(g)(1)); and notice of a change in the amount of a recurring charge no less than 7 and no more than 30 days before it takes effect (17602(g)(2)). Cards cite this as the "material change notice" rule. Verify at: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17602
- California Automatic Renewal Law, free trials: a free gift or trial longer than 31 days that converts to a paid renewal needs a notice at least 3 and at most 21 days before it ends (17602(b)(1)(A)). Verify at: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17602
- New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection subscription-cancellation rule, in effect from 1 October 2026: material terms shown before consent, cancellation as straightforward as sign-up and through the same method, an online cancellation path when consent was taken in person, and a notice of an upcoming charge 3 to 21 days before the cancellation deadline, with how to cancel. Civil penalties start at 525 US dollars per violation. Verify at: https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/rule/cancellation-of-subscriptions/

## Pricing, subscriptions, and switching in the EU

- Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC, Articles 6 and 7: a commercial practice is misleading when it gives false or deceptive information, or omits material information, about the main characteristics of a product (which includes services and digital content) or about the price or how it is calculated. Verify at: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj
- Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 6(1)(e): before a distance contract, the trader gives the total price including taxes; for a contract of indeterminate duration or a subscription, the total costs per billing period, and, at a fixed rate, the total monthly costs. It covers services and digital content. Verify at: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj
- Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC: the selling price (and the unit price) of goods is shown to consumers. Article 6a, added by the Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161 and applying from 28 May 2022, requires a price-reduction announcement to show the prior price, the lowest price in at least the 30 days before. The Commission's guidance reads the PID, including Article 6a, as covering goods only, not services or digital content; for software subscriptions cite the UCPD and the CRD instead. Verify at: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1998/6/oj and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(06)
- EU Data Act, Regulation (EU) 2023/2854: applies from 12 September 2025. Chapter VI (Articles 23 to 31) governs switching between data processing services, which include software as a service. The contract must let the customer switch and export all exportable data, with a notice period of at most 2 months, a transitional period of at most 30 calendar days, and at least 30 days to retrieve the data (Article 25). Article 31 exempts services mostly custom-built for one customer and time-limited test versions from some of these duties. Verify at: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj
- EU Data Act, switching charges (Article 29): until 12 January 2027 a provider may charge only reduced, cost-based switching and egress charges, disclosed in advance; from 12 January 2027 no switching charges are allowed. The Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal of 19 November 2025 would amend the switching rules; it was still a proposal at review (verify current status). Verify at: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj

## Stripe Billing

- Subscription statuses: `incomplete` (first payment not yet made; the customer has 23 hours), `incomplete_expired` (the first payment never succeeded; no billing), `trialing`, `active`, `past_due` (the latest invoice failed or was not attempted), `canceled` (terminal), `unpaid` (set only when the Dashboard's failed-payment settings choose it; Stripe says to revoke access), and `paused` (a trial ended with no payment method and the end behavior is pause). Whether a subscription ends `past_due`, `canceled`, or `unpaid` after the last retry is a Dashboard setting. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/overview
- Subscription lifecycle events: `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated` (renewals, plan changes, coupons, and other changes, including those made in the customer portal), `customer.subscription.deleted` (the subscription ended), `customer.subscription.paused`, `customer.subscription.resumed`, `customer.subscription.trial_will_end` (3 days before a trial ends, or at once for a shorter trial), `invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`, `invoice.payment_action_required`, `invoice.upcoming` (a few days before renewal, as set in the Dashboard; not sent for `send_invoice` billing), `invoice.finalization_failed`, and `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated`. Refunds, disputes, and fraud warnings arrive as `charge.refunded`, `charge.dispute.created`, and `radar.early_fraud_warning.created`. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/webhooks
- A webhook endpoint receives only the event types enabled on it. That list is set in the Dashboard (Workbench) or through the API, not in the handler code, so a handler branch for an event the endpoint does not receive never runs. Stripe advises enabling only the events the integration needs. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks
- Checkout fulfillment: Stripe requires a webhook (`checkout.session.completed`, plus `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` for delayed payment methods) because customers may never reach the success page; webhooks are required for subscriptions. The success page may also trigger fulfillment, but only through a server function that retrieves the Checkout Session and checks its `payment_status`, and that is safe to run more than once. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment
- API keys: sandbox (test) keys start `pk_test_`, `rk_test_`, and `sk_test_`; live keys start `pk_live_`, `rk_live_`, and `sk_live_`. Each mode has its own keys and objects, and objects carry `livemode`. Stripe now calls its test environments sandboxes. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/keys
- Prices: a price's amount cannot change after it is created (only fields such as `active`, `nickname`, `metadata`, and `lookup_key`); a new amount means a new price. Archiving a price (`active=false`) leaves existing subscriptions on it until they are canceled, so existing subscribers keep the old price unless code or a script moves them. A `lookup_key` lets the pricing page and checkout read the same price, and `transfer_lookup_key=true` moves the key to a new price for new purchases. Checkout picks the customer's local currency from the IP address when the price has `currency_options` for it, else the price's default currency. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/products-prices/manage-prices
- API version 2025-03-31.basil moved `current_period_start` and `current_period_end` from the subscription to each subscription item (`items.data[].current_period_end`), and an invoice reaches its subscription through `parent.subscription_details.subscription`. Code written for an older pinned version reads the old fields; check the version the client pins before filing a missing-field finding. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/basil/2025-03-31/deprecate-subscription-current-period-start-and-end
- Failed payments: Smart Retries or custom retry rules, the customer emails about failed payments, and the final status after retries are Dashboard settings (revenue recovery), not code. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries
- Tax: Stripe Tax calculates tax in Checkout when the session sets `automatic_tax[enabled]=true`, and only in jurisdictions where the account has an active tax registration. Without it, Checkout adds tax only from tax rates the code passes, except under a Stripe preview that turns automatic tax on from the Dashboard for one-time Checkout payments. A missing flag in code is therefore a question for Scope and limitations, not proof that no tax is collected. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/tax/checkout/page
- Usage-based billing: a meter event carries an `identifier`; Stripe enforces its uniqueness within a rolling period of at least 24 hours and generates one when none is sent, so a retry without a stable identifier is billed twice. Event timestamps must be within the past 35 days. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/api/billing/meter-event/create
- Entitlements: features are attached to products; Stripe keeps an active entitlement per customer and feature, lists them at `GET /v1/entitlements/active_entitlements?customer=<id>`, and sends `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated` when they change. A feature added to or removed from a product reaches existing subscriptions at the start of their next billing period. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/entitlements
- Plan changes: a subscription update that changes a price or quantity takes effect at once and, by default (`proration_behavior=create_prorations`), adds proration items to the next invoice; `always_invoice` bills the difference immediately and `none` skips proration. Negative prorations are not refunded automatically. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/prorations
- Cancellation: `cancel_at_period_end` keeps the subscription, and its access, until the period ends; a `canceled` subscription cannot be updated except its metadata and `cancellation_details`. `cancellation_details.feedback` takes `customer_service`, `low_quality`, `missing_features`, `other`, `switched_service`, `too_complex`, `too_expensive`, or `unused`, and `cancellation_details.comment` holds free text. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/object

## Merchants of record and other payment providers

- Paddle Billing acts as merchant of record: Paddle computes, collects, and remits sales tax and VAT and issues the invoices. Its sandbox API host is `sandbox-api.paddle.com` (live: `api.paddle.com`), its sandbox dashboard is `sandbox-vendors.paddle.com`, sandbox API keys contain `_sdbx`, and sandbox client-side tokens start `test_` (live tokens start `live_`). Verify at: https://www.paddle.com/billing and https://developer.paddle.com/sdks/sandbox/
- Paddle.js runs in sandbox only when the page calls `Paddle.Environment.set("sandbox")` before `Paddle.Initialize()`; without the call it uses production. Verify at: https://developer.paddle.com/paddle-js/methods/paddle-environment-set/
- Paddle Billing webhook events: `subscription.created`, `subscription.activated`, `subscription.trialing`, `subscription.updated` (also sent for renewals, plan changes, pauses, resumes, and cancellations), `subscription.past_due`, `subscription.paused`, `subscription.resumed`, `subscription.canceled`, `subscription.imported`, and the transaction events `transaction.completed`, `transaction.paid`, `transaction.payment_failed`, `transaction.past_due`, `transaction.billed`, `transaction.canceled`, `transaction.created`, `transaction.ready`, `transaction.revised`, `transaction.updated`, and the adjustment events `adjustment.created` and `adjustment.updated`. Refunds, credits, chargebacks, and chargeback reversals arrive as adjustments (the adjustment's `action` is `refund`, `credit`, `chargeback`, `chargeback_reverse`, and others); most refunds are created `pending_approval` and reach `approved` or `rejected` through `adjustment.updated`, while credits are created approved, so a handler that names no adjustment event never sees a refund or a chargeback. Verify at: https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/overview and https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/adjustments/adjustment-created
- Lemon Squeezy acts as merchant of record: it collects and remits sales tax and VAT on the seller's behalf. Test mode uses its own API keys, which reach test data only, and a webhook created in test mode has `test_mode: true`. Webhook events: `order_created`, `order_refunded`, `subscription_created`, `subscription_updated`, `subscription_cancelled`, `subscription_resumed`, `subscription_expired`, `subscription_paused`, `subscription_unpaused`, `subscription_payment_success`, `subscription_payment_failed`, `subscription_payment_recovered`, `subscription_payment_refunded`, and the license key, customer, and affiliate events. Verify at: https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/merchant-of-record and https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/test-mode and https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/webhooks/event-types
- Polar acts as merchant of record and calculates, collects, and remits tax. Its sandbox is a separate environment: dashboard `sandbox.polar.sh`, API host `sandbox-api.polar.sh` (live: `api.polar.sh`), separate access tokens, and SDKs that select it with `environment: "sandbox"`. Verify at: https://polar.sh/docs/integrate/sandbox
- PayPal REST API hosts: sandbox `api-m.sandbox.paypal.com`, live `api-m.paypal.com`; each environment has its own client id and secret. Verify at: https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/requests/
- Braintree: the sandbox runs on `sandbox.braintreegateway.com` and `api.sandbox.braintreegateway.com`, is not linked to production, and SDKs select it with `Environment.SANDBOX` (production: `Environment.PRODUCTION`). Verify at: https://developer.paypal.com/braintree/articles/get-started/try-it-out
- Adyen Checkout API hosts: test `checkout-test.adyen.com`; live `<prefix>-checkout-live.adyenpayments.com`, with a prefix unique to the merchant account. Verify at: https://docs.adyen.com/api-explorer/Checkout/latest/overview

In the United States storefront, apps may include buttons, external links, and other calls to action to other ways of buying; the guidelines changed after the Epic v. Apple order of 30 April 2025. In other storefronts such calls to action stay barred except under Apple's regional programs, and EU storefronts follow Apple's Digital Markets Act business terms. - Epic v. - Google Play Real-time developer notifications, subscription types: `SUBSCRIPTION_RECOVERED` (1), `SUBSCRIPTION_RENEWED` (2), `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELED` (3), `SUBSCRIPTION_PURCHASED` (4), `SUBSCRIPTION_ON_HOLD` (5), `SUBSCRIPTION_IN_GRACE_PERIOD` (6), `SUBSCRIPTION_RESTARTED` (7), `SUBSCRIPTION_DEFERRED` (9), `SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSED` (10), `SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSE_SCHEDULE_CHANGED` (11), `SUBSCRIPTION_REVOKED` (12), `SUBSCRIPTION_EXPIRED` (13), `SUBSCRIPTION_ITEMS_CHANGED` (17), `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLATION_SCHEDULED` (18), `SUBSCRIPTION_PRICE_CHANGE_UPDATED` (19), `SUBSCRIPTION_PENDING_PURCHASE_CANCELED` (20), and `SUBSCRIPTION_PRICE_STEP_UP_CONSENT_UPDATED` (22). One-time products send `ONE_TIME_PRODUCT_PURCHASED` and `ONE_TIME_PRODUCT_CANCELED`, and refunds arrive as a `VoidedPurchaseNotification`. - Google Play Payments policy: apps use Google Play's billing system for digital goods, subscriptions, and app functionality, with exceptions for physical goods and services and for enrolled alternative-billing and external-offer programs in eligible regions. ## Business metric definitions

- Stripe Billing analytics, MRR: the sum of the monthly-normalized value of all `active` and `past_due` subscriptions (a 600 US dollar yearly price counts 50 a month). It excludes taxes, free plans, metered usage, and subscriptions or items in a trial; a subscription that is canceled or marked `unpaid` counts as churn. Whether discounts are subtracted is a Dashboard setting, except permanent recurring discounts, which are always subtracted. Active subscribers are those with positive MRR in `active` or `past_due`, one per customer; subscriber churn rate is churned subscribers in the past 30 days divided by active subscribers 30 days ago plus new subscribers in the past 30 days. A team may define its metrics differently; KPI cards judge a definition against its own label and docs, and use this one only when the code or a comment says it matches Stripe. Verify at: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/analytics

## Product analytics: event deduplication ids

- Amplitude HTTP V2 API: Amplitude drops an event that repeats an `insert_id` already seen for the same `device_id` in the same project within the past 7 days. Verify at: https://amplitude.com/docs/apis/analytics/http-v2
- Mixpanel: events with the same event name, `distinct_id`, `time`, and `$insert_id` are duplicates, and reports show one; an event sent without `$insert_id` gets a generated id that does not qualify for deduplication. Raw exports are not deduplicated. Verify at: https://docs.mixpanel.com/reference/event-deduplication
- Segment: the API deduplicates on `messageId`, with a look-back window of at least 24 hours. Verify at: https://segment.com/docs/guides/duplicate-data/
- PostHog: events with the same `uuid`, event name, timestamp, and `distinct_id` are eventually deduplicated (during background merges); server-side code that sends no `uuid` cannot be deduplicated. Verify at: https://posthog.com/docs/data/events
- Google Analytics 4: purchase events with the same `transaction_id` are deduplicated, for web data streams only; an empty `transaction_id` makes every such purchase a duplicate. Verify at: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12313109

## Versioning and deprecation

- RFC 9745 (March 2025, Standards Track) defines the `Deprecation` response header, whose value is a Structured Field date (`Deprecation: @1688169599`), and the `deprecation` link relation to human-readable deprecation docs. A `Sunset` date must not be earlier than the `Deprecation` date. Verify at: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9745
- RFC 8594 (May 2019, Informational) defines the `Sunset` response header, whose value is an HTTP-date (`Sunset: Sat, 31 Dec 2018 23:59:59 GMT`), and the `sunset` link relation to the retirement policy. Verify at: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8594
- Semantic Versioning 2.0.0: rule 8 requires a new major version for any backward-incompatible change to the public API; rule 4 says that under major version zero (0.y.z) anything may change at any time. Verify at: https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html
- Keep a Changelog 1.1.0 groups changes under Added, Changed, Deprecated, Removed, Fixed, and Security, with the newest version first. Verify at: https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/
